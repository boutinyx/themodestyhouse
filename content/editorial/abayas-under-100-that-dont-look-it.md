Price is not what gives an abaya away. I've seen $200 abayas that look like a costume and $40 ones I've had to ask about. What gives it away is always the same short list of things, and once you know them you can shop the bottom of the market without it showing.

## What actually makes an abaya look cheap

**Shine instead of texture.** Flat, high-gloss polyester catches light in one hard sheet. Fabric with something happening in it — a crepe with etched lines, a slub cotton, pleating — breaks the light up and reads as fabric someone chose.

**No closures.** A cheap abaya is a shape with sleeves. The moment you see a hidden back zip, an adjustable tie, a real placket, you are looking at something that was patterned rather than cut.

**No pockets.** This is the fastest tell there is. Pockets are extra pattern pieces and extra labour, and they are the first thing cut from a budget garment.

**Nothing at the edges.** Cuffs, hem, neckline. A plain finish is fine; an unfinished one isn't. Lace at the cuff, a tassel at the neck, a trim marking where one fabric becomes another — those are the cheapest possible ways to make a garment look considered, and most low-priced abayas skip them anyway.
