## Executive Summary

The site's long-form copy — `/about`, `/faq`, category explainers — is genuinely excellent:
specific, honest, differentiated, and written in a real editorial voice. That's the biggest
strength, and it's real, not audit flattery: three independent subagents (content,
competitive, brand) converged on the same finding without being told to look for it.

The biggest gap is that this strength never reaches the surfaces a visitor actually sees
first. The hero sells *aggregation* ("The archive for everything modest," "Every modest
brand, in one house") — the exact thing `marketing-strategy-vs-mdst.md` says not to compete
on — while the real differentiator ("chosen by hand," the exclusion list, the seal
methodology) is written well but buried below the fold or on pages nobody lands on directly.
Nobody is named anywhere on the site, despite a strong first-person voice already existing in
`/about` and both editorial posts.

The second-biggest finding, found **independently by three of the five subagents** (content,
conversion, and brand/strategy — none were told to look for it), is a live factual error:
the FAQ states prices are "shown in each brand's own currency… we don't convert," but the
site has defaulted to USD approximations since 2026-08-12. On a site whose entire product is
trustworthiness, a trust-copy page contradicting the trust behavior of the page next to it is
the single highest-priority fix in this report.

Growth & Strategy scored lowest (42/100) for an honest reason, not a design flaw: Skimlinks
is still a stub, so the site is pre-revenue by construction. No dollar-impact estimates are
given anywhere in this report — there is no live monetization to estimate against, and
inventing a number would violate this project's own rule against invented figures.

**Top 3 actions that would move the needle most:**
1. Fix the FAQ/homepage currency and seal-claim contradictions (today — it's a few sentences).
2. Name a real person on `/about` and byline the two editorial posts (this week — the voice
   already exists, it just isn't attributed).

## The convergent finding: fix the currency/FAQ contradiction

Found independently by 3 of 5 subagents, from different angles (copy review, live conversion
click-path review, and trust/legal review). This is the single most-verified finding in the
report.

**The problem:** `/faq` Q8 states *"Prices are shown in each brand's own currency, since
that's what you'd actually pay on their site — we don't convert or mark them up."* The live
site (`/directory`, `/modest-dresses`) renders `≈ $167`, `≈ $91`, header `"$ USD"` — USD
approximation became the default on 2026-08-12 (ADR-0002 supersession, CLAUDE.md §8). The
copy was never updated after the behavior changed.

**Suggested fix** (content subagent's draft, yours to edit):
> *"We show an approximate USD conversion by default, marked with ≈, so you can compare
> across houses. Switch to the brand's own currency any time — that's the figure you'll
> actually be charged. We never mark anything up."*

Same correction applies to the homepage footer disclaimer, which makes the same claim.

**Related, same root cause (assertion vs. evidence), lower urgency:** the homepage claims
every label "passed our review for craft, sizing and ethics." `/about` says 5 of 112 houses
carry the seal — "ethics" isn't defined or evidenced anywhere. Unify the standard's wording
across the homepage seal band, the 3-step apply block, and `/about` (currently worded three
different ways), and either substantiate or drop "ethics."

## Detailed Analysis by Category

### Content & Messaging — 72/100

Docked for: the FAQ/currency contradiction (see above); the seal standard worded three

and design"); a homepage claim of universal review that `/about`'s own numbers (5 of 112)
contradict; and an H1 ("The archive for everything modest") that's passive and fails the
5-second test against the site's own, sharper subhead.

### Conversion Optimization — 68/100

already wired. What's missing is anything *visual* signaling the click leaves the site, a
price filter on a 15k-item catalogue, and adequate mobile tap targets on the quick-view/
favorite buttons (32px, in the tap path of the primary action).

**UNVERIFIED (WebFetch can't drive JS):** filter dropdown usability on touch, the quick-view
modal's actual contents, the currency switcher, "Load more" behavior, and a real tap-through

### SEO & Discoverability — 72/100
