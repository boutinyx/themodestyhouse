## Findings

### 1. The format reaches strangers; nothing happens next
66.4% non-follower views replicates the reel pattern already measured twice (75.0%, 76.4% on
7 Sep and 30 Aug). **Distribution is not the problem with this format.** What is new and bad:
**0 saves, 0 shares, 0 comments, 0 profile visits, 0 follows** — where the two earlier reels
took 4 and 2 saves. A 20-second reel naming four brands, watched mostly by strangers,
produced no onward action of any kind.

### 2. The video is letterboxed
The reel plays with black bars top and bottom — roughly a 16:9 image inside a 9:16 slot,
which spends about 40% of a phone screen on nothing. This is the cheapest fix available and
it is a render setting, not a creative decision.

### 3. The grid has no single visual signature
Five distinct visual registers across 13 tiles: photographic editorial, AI illustration,
flat-lay product collage, typographic cover, and now a 3D-rendered set. The flat-lays and the
typographic covers are each internally consistent and on-brand; nothing ties the five families
to each other. Since **the profile is where conversion actually happens** (finding 4), this is
a conversion problem, not a taste one.

### 4. The funnel's missing middle
Two first-party facts that only reconcile one way:
- Pulse, last 30 days: Instagram sends **151 people, 30% of all site traffic**.
- Post Insights: **0 external link taps on every post ever measured**, including this one.

So site traffic arrives via **profile → bio link**, never from a post. The metric that
predicts traffic is therefore **profile visits**, and the pilot scored 0.

### 5. The largest unused lever is collaborator posts
108 brands in the directory; the account has never coauthored a post. @mdst_market's archive
(2026-08-28): coauthored posts median **77 likes against 31 solo**, used on only 8 of 138
posts. The "New in the House" format is built for it — it features four named brands.

### 6. The caption is inverted
It opens on four brand names the audience does not know. The measured pattern across three
accounts is that **posts stating the problem travel and posts stating the product do not**
(`docs/instagram-account-review-2026-09-07.md` §3.3). It also asks nothing: 22 posts, still
zero comments.

### 7. Hashtags are too large to rank in
`#modest #modesty #modestfashion #hijab` are all enormous. A post with 5 likes cannot surface
in any of them.

## Notes / follow-ups
- **The pilot was 10 hours old when read.** Re-read it in a week before treating any of its
  zeroes as settled.
- **9 posts were not read** — archived or hidden behind the visible grid. Stated rather than
  quietly excluded (§10.52).
- The second post published since 9 Sep was not opened individually.
- No recommendation here has been acted on; this is a read.

# Addendum — grid coherence and hashtag sizing

## The best comparable account runs ONE format, 238 times
`@thevirtualedits` — 17,700 followers, median 184 likes, **1.04% engagement**, the highest of
the three accounts compared on 2026-09-07. Its grid is a single visual format on every tile:
a flat-lay outfit collage on a pale ground. Nothing else, for 238 posts.

Its captions carry only five hashtags, all enormous: `#ootd #fyp #instagram #modest #fashion`.
A sampled post with 44 likes tags three brands in the post header (`massimodutti`, `cosstores`,

## Recommendation recorded
- **Three formats, each with a fixed template**, and nothing else: the reel (salon set), the
  flat-lay collage (parchment ground, monogram), the typographic statement (aubergine field,
  Bodoni). Drop the stock-photo editorial and the AI illustration — neither is ownable and
  neither repeats.
- **One unifying rule:** every tile carries aubergine or parchment, and the monogram sits in
  the same corner on every tile.
- **Hashtag ladder** rather than four giants: 2 large for topic classification, 3 mid,
  4 small where a 20-like post can actually rank, plus one Turkish tag since the reels are
  landing in Turkey and North Africa (`docs/log/2026-09-09-who-is-actually-reached.md`,
  second addendum).

# Second addendum — Pinterest: the 105k is impressions on pins with no destination
**Read:** 2026-09-12, from Tina's logged-in Pinterest, after she asked why Pulse shows almost

## The account

```
UITGAANDE KLIKKEN 0 · Volgacties 0 · Profielbezoeken 0 (-100%)
```

**"Klikken op pin" is not a click to the site** — it expands the pin. The metric that sends
traffic is **Uitgaande klikken**, and it is **0**.

## Three structural faults, all visible in the business hub
1. **`themodestyhouse.com` is not claimed.** The hub offers "themodestyhouse.com claimen" as
   an outstanding action. Unclaimed means no attribution on pins of her content and no domain
   analytics.
2. **The Pinterest tag is not installed.** The hub prompts "Installeer de Pinterest-tag".
   Nothing on the site can report a Pinterest conversion.
3. **The pins carry no destination URL and no description.** All fourteen are titled exactly
   `Hijabi outfit`.

Account-level analytics (`analytics.pinterest.com/overview/`) returned
"Niet genoeg gegevens om weer te geven" on every panel, which is consistent with 1 and 2.

## Reading
Pinterest pays out in outbound clicks. 105k impressions against **0 outbound clicks** on the
pin measured is not an attribution gap — there is nothing to attribute. The 13 visits Pulse
recorded in 30 days are people who went pin → profile → bio link, the long way round.

The good news is in the saves: **363 and 325 saves** on the two best pins. Saves are
Pinterest's strongest ranking signal and the content is clearly working. It simply points
nowhere. One commenter asked, under a pin, *"Girl what is your insta ????"* — demand with no
route.

## Notes / follow-ups
- Those pins appear to be other people's outfit photographs. Pointing them at a commercial
  site is a different use from a personal board — same open rights question as the brand
  product photos in `docs/tiktok/HANDOFF.md`.
- Nothing was changed. The domain was not claimed, no tag was installed, no pin was edited.
