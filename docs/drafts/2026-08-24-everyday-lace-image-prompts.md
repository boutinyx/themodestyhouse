# Everyday Lace — image generation prompts
**Date:** 2026-08-24 · For: the `/edits/everyday-lace` hero + outfit-idea images

Two jobs, and they want opposite treatments:
- **The hero** — one image, editorial, has to say "lace" in half a second.
- **The outfit ideas** — many images, Instagram-flavoured, each teaching one way to wear it.

Every prompt below bakes in the rules from the edit's own styling copy, because an outfit
image that contradicts the text underneath it is worse than no image:
**lace goes against structure, never against soft. Denim is soft-vs-hard. Contrast makes
the lace visible — black lace on white, not black on black.**

Sizes to generate at: **16:9 for the hero** (desktop) and **5:8 portrait** for the phone
crop — both measured off the reference on 2026-08-24, they are not interchangeable.
Outfit ideas: **4:5 portrait**, the Instagram feed ratio.

---

## 1. THE HERO — 16:9

> Editorial fashion photograph, wide 16:9. A woman in a hijab seen from the chest up and
> slightly turned away, wearing a cream lace blouse with a wide scalloped lace edge running
> across the collarbone. The lace is the subject: shot close enough that the individual
> threads and the open pattern are unmistakable, backlit so light passes through the holes
> in the lace and throws a faint pattern on the skin beneath. Behind her, a plain warm
> parchment wall, thrown far out of focus. Deep aubergine and cream palette, one soft
> window light from the left, no hard shadow. Shot on 85mm, shallow depth of field, natural
> skin texture, no retouching gloss. Calm, expensive, quiet. Leave the middle third
> uncluttered for a title.
>
> Negative: no text, no logo, no jewellery on the neck, no busy background, no harsh flash,
> no oversaturation, no visible hair, no sheer or clinging fabric.

**Why it is framed this way.** The instruction "show immediately that we're talking about
lace" is a scale problem, not a styling one — lace read at full-body distance is texture
you cannot identify. Backlight through the holes is the one lighting choice that makes lace
legible as *lace* rather than as pattern. The empty middle third is for the title, which
sits centred over the photograph.

**Phone version — 5:8 portrait.** Re-generate rather than crop: the same shot, framed
vertically, the lace edge running diagonally across the upper third so it survives a tall
crop.

## 2. OUTFIT IDEAS — 4:5 portrait, Instagram

House style to keep constant across all of them, so they read as a set:

> Full-length or three-quarter outfit photograph of a hijabi woman, natural daylight, plain
> warm parchment or off-white wall, shot on 50mm at eye level, standing naturally. Warm
> neutral palette. Real fabric texture. No text, no logo, no face in sharp focus.
> Negative: no clinging fabric, no sheer panels, no visible hair, no exposed arms above the
> wrist, no bare legs, no harsh flash, no clutter.

### a. The lace belt, worn as a mini skirt
> Wide black lace belt worn low over a long straight cream column dress, so the lace band
> sits like a short skirt over the top of it. Long sleeves. The dress is smooth and matte;
> the lace is the only texture in the frame. Black on cream, maximum contrast.

### b. The lace belt with a long sleeve
> Cream lace belt cinched at the waist over a plain black long-sleeve maxi dress. The dress
> is structured and falls in one straight line. The belt is the only ornament.

### c. Lace skirt + denim
> A black lace midi skirt with a rigid indigo denim jacket, buttoned. Soft against hard —
> the denim stiff and square-shouldered, the lace fluid underneath. Plain black top between
> them. Nothing else.

### d. Lace abaya, open, over plain
> An open-front cream lace abaya worn over a plain black column dress underneath, so the
> dark base shows through the lace pattern and makes it legible. Straight sleeves, no
> gather, no frill.

### e. Lace hijab, everyday
> Close three-quarter portrait. A plain jersey abaya in warm taupe with a modal hijab whose
> only detail is a narrow scalloped lace edge along the front. Understated — the lace is a
> trim, not a statement. Daylight, no shadow.

### f. Lace sleeves
> A plain structured black midi dress with full-length sheer-lace sleeves in the same black.
> Everything else in the outfit is flat and matte, so the sleeves are the only thing with
> texture.

### g. Sleeveless lace dress under a blazer
> A slim sleeveless lace slip dress worn as a layer under a sharply tailored cream blazer,
> with a plain long-sleeve top underneath the dress for coverage. The blazer is crisp and
> structured; the lace dress moves. Soft against hard.

### h. Contrast, stated plainly
> Two-image diptych, same model, same pose, same light. Left: black lace top with white
> wide-leg trousers. Right: the identical black lace top with black trousers. The pair is
> the point — the left reads as lace instantly, the right disappears.

## Notes
- **(a) and (b) are aspirational, not shoppable.** Checked the catalogue on 2026-08-24: the
  directory has **zero** lace belts. One hit, BAQA's "Cord Belt Detailed Lace Blouse", is a
  blouse. Same for the slip-dress idea in (g) — two pieces in the whole directory. Worth
  knowing before these are used as if they point at stock.
- (h) is the only one that needs two frames. It is also the one that teaches the most.
- Generate a couple of variants of each and pick — lace is the thing generators most often
  render as printed pattern rather than as open, holed fabric. **Reject any image where you
  cannot see through the lace.** That is the single check that matters.
