You know the look even if you've never named it. Undecorated. Slightly too big. Brown, grey, oatmeal, never quite black. A jersey scarf that's clearly good but isn't trying to tell you so. Trousers, not an abaya. It reads as *nothing special* until you try to copy it and can't work out which part you're missing.

I got curious enough to actually measure it. There are thirteen Dutch and Flemish houses in this directory — Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Nijmegen, Arnhem, Antwerp — and between them, 2,341 pieces. I put those next to the other 17,740 from everywhere else and looked at what they do differently.

It turns out to be five things, and almost all of them are subtractions.

> **YOUR BIT — the opening.** You live here. Is this a real, recognisable thing to you, or is it a thing outsiders see and Dutch girls would never name? Either answer makes the piece better — the second one makes it funnier.

## 1. Jersey. Five times more jersey.

This is the big one and it isn't close. **27.6% of everything the Dutch houses make is jersey, against 5.4% for the rest of the directory — 5.1x.**

That's the whole foundation. Not chiffon, not silk, not anything that catches light. A fabric that holds its shape, doesn't slide, doesn't need a pin, and looks the same at 9pm as it did at 8am. It's the least glamorous choice available and they've built an entire aesthetic on it.

It's also, incidentally, what the rest of Europe is only now catching up to. *Liquid jersey hijab* is up **950% in UK search** this year — and when I looked at who actually sells it, the three biggest are **Losyana** in Nijmegen, **Merrachi** in Amsterdam and **Noureen** in Antwerp. Britain is discovering a fabric the Netherlands has been wearing on ordinary days for years.

One more thing about the timing: of every fabric in modest fashion, jersey is the only one that's both fast-growing *and* big. It's twenty-five times the size of lace as a search term, and — going back five years — **August is its highest month of the year**. This look is peaking as you read it.

> **YOUR BIT — jersey.** What separates a good jersey scarf from a bad one? Weight, grip, the way it holds a fold? This is the single most useful paragraph you could write for anyone trying to copy the look.

## 2. Nothing on it

**4.7x less embroidery. 3.1x less lace.** Where the rest of the directory adds — beadwork, stonework, laser-cut trim — these houses just don't.

If you've ever bought something to get this look and it came out wrong, this is probably why. It wasn't the shape. It was that yours had a detail on it.

## 3. Separates, not an abaya

Everyone else's catalogue is 28% abayas. The Dutch houses are **14% — half.** What replaces it is hijab (37% of everything they make, against 24%), plus trousers, skirts and tops.

So it isn't one garment you throw on. It's an outfit, assembled, with the scarf treated as the main event rather than the finishing touch. Also **7.3x less tunic** and **4.2x less open-front kimono** — the two silhouettes that dominate everywhere else are close to absent here.

## 4. Oversized, on purpose

**2.6x more.** Not "modest so it's loose" — deliberately, visibly a size up, which is a completely different intention and reads completely differently.

## 5. Brown. Grey. Never black.

The only houses in the directory that use **less** black than average: **1.4x less.** What's there instead is brown, mocha and camel at **1.6x**, grey at **1.6x**, olive and sage at 1.4x, cream and taupe at 1.3x.

Warm neutrals, and enough of them to be a rule rather than a coincidence. If you want one shortcut into this whole thing: stop buying black.

> **YOUR BIT — colour.** Which browns actually work together, and which combination looks cheap? Data can tell people to buy brown. It can't tell them which brown.

## The odd one out

**2.2x more swimwear.** I don't have a tidy story for this one and I'm not going to invent one — but it's real, it's the Dutch houses doing it, and Merrachi alone lists 33 swim pieces. Make of it what you like.

> **YOUR BIT — your version.** Five outfits, or one week, built from these rules — this is where the title earns itself. It's called a diary; give it a diary.

## So, the recipe

Jersey. Nothing on it. Separates with the scarf leading. One size up. Brown instead of black.

That's it. Five decisions, four of which are refusals. Which is why it's hard to copy from a photo — you're looking for the thing that makes it work, and the thing that makes it work is everything they left out.
